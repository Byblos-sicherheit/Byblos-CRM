# Worked Example: Develocity Closing Argument

This is a reusable example, not a source of permanent product truth. Refresh all product claims against current official sources before using it externally.

## Why Develocity, Why Now

AI has changed the economics of software creation. Code can now be generated at machine speed, but every generated change still has to be understood, built, tested, governed, integrated, and released. The constraint has moved. The organizations that win will not be those that generate the most code; they will be those that can convert more change into reliable production outcomes without allowing cost, risk, and review burden to grow at the same rate.

Develocity should be evaluated as the control and context layer for that new operating model. It sits between the toolchain and the humans and AI agents working inside it, turning build, test, dependency, policy, and provenance signals into structured evidence. That evidence can be queried, analyzed, governed, and acted on rather than reconstructed from logs after a failure.

The governance case is direct. As agents create more artifacts, leadership needs to know what was built, from which inputs, through which process, under which policy, and whether the evidence survives after the ephemeral build environment disappears. Provenance and policy controls convert AI-assisted delivery from an informal productivity experiment into an accountable production system.

The efficiency case is equally important. AI agents multiply builds, tests, dependency resolution, retries, and failure diagnosis. Without acceleration, the cost curve rises with agent volume. Shared caching, artifact caching, selective testing, test distribution, flaky-test control, and structured failure context reduce repeated work and shorten the validation loop. This protects both developer capacity and infrastructure spend.

The software-delivery case is measurable through DORA. Shorter and more reliable feedback supports smaller changes and more frequent deployment. Trustworthy tests and policy gates reduce escaped failures. Faster diagnosis and rebuild cycles shorten recovery. The value is not a single faster build; it is speed and stability improving together across the delivery system.

The ROI should be measured against the organization's own baseline: engineering hours lost to waiting and diagnosis, CI compute and egress, dependency-repository load, rerun rates, audit effort, incident rework, and the percentage of AI-generated changes that actually reach production. Develocity's business case is the combination of reclaimed capacity, avoided infrastructure cost, more reliable releases, and governance that scales with delivery volume.

The recommendation is to adopt through a controlled, measurable pilot on representative high-volume repositories. Establish current feedback, failure, cost, provenance, and DORA baselines; enable the relevant Develocity capabilities; measure the delta; and make the enterprise rollout decision from observed results. The strategic risk is not moving too early. It is scaling AI-generated change on a delivery system designed for human-scale throughput and discovering too late that validation, governance, and CI economics have become the limiting factors.
